Applications

Every application arrives finished.

The product is the finished thing an employee opens on Monday morning: a catalog of named applications, delivered governed, kept current across your fleet, and working the day they land. Your teams use them without anyone on your staff assembling them.

Talk to us The standard library

The standard library, included in every deployment.

Three applications cover what most employees need from day one. They ship as proven, named applications integrated under our app contract, with licenses honored, and they behave as one product because a single governed memory layer sits underneath all of them.

01 · Chat & content

Enterprise chat and content creation.

The surface most employees touch every day: the chat experience your team already wants from consumer AI tools, on models your company chose, under rules your company set.

  • Runs against local models, or frontier providers under your own keys with allowances.
  • Group-level model assignment: each team gets the model that fits its work.
  • Prompts and threads stay in your perimeter unless a labelled, governed crossing says otherwise.
In the console

Company chat

Powered by Llama 3.3 70B, hosted in your perimeter · all groups · in perimeter

Optional: frontier escalation under your key, per-person allowance, automatic fallback to the hosted model when spent.

02 · Retrieval

Retrieval over your own corpus.

What makes it your AI: answers grounded in your own documents, policies, contracts, and wikis. We deliberately scope this to "ingest your sources and retrieval works": a finished capability, sized so it runs without an engineering team behind it.

  • Corpus bindings are configured at deployment and evolve as your sources do.
  • Your documents never leave your perimeter; retrieval runs inside it.
  • Access follows your groups: people only retrieve from sources they're allowed to see.
In the console

Knowledge assistant

Corpus: policies, contracts, wiki · finance + legal groups · in perimeter

Sources are bound per app; the sovereignty score shows exactly what each app can reach.

03 · Coding

A private coding assistant, in the IDE your developers already use.

Often the most acute unmet need: mainstream coding assistants phone home, so security teams block them, and developers lose the tool they want most. Freehold exposes a governed model endpoint to the IDE of choice, so code and prompts stay under your control.

  • High-value, high-volume, sustained work: exactly the load that justifies owned capacity over time.
  • Benefits from controlled egress: live docs and package lookups are permitted, logged, and labelled.
  • Same gateway, same budgets, same audit trail as every other app.
In the console

Coding assistant

Powered by Qwen2.5-Coder-32B · IDE endpoint · engineering group · governed egress

Egress: docs and package registries, whitelisted and logged. Everything else stays in.

One memory, shared by every app.

What turns three separate applications into one product: a member can start a thread in chat and pick the same context back up in the coding assistant, because both sit on the same governed, in-perimeter memory store.

Governed once

Curated at the connector layer

Memory is wired once, beneath every app, instead of per-app integrations multiplying with each addition. Adding an app never silently adds a new place your data lives.

Member-owned

Retention, deletion, export

Every connector that touches member data carries the same obligations: retention limits, clean deletion when a member offboards, export on request, and no administrator back-door into member content.

In-perimeter

Even when the model isn't

Member context is held inside your boundary even when the model answering a given request is an external provider reached under your key.

Know exactly what's on your shelf.

Add-on applications are selected at deployment and added as your needs grow. Every app carries a publisher label, so you always know who's behind the code, and the console pairs each app with its observed adoption, so you can see which ones earn their keep.

Publisher labelWhat it meansWhat you should expect
First-party Built by Freehold, for the catalog. We are the vendor and support path, end to end.
Adopted open source A proven open-source application, integrated under the app contract. Licenses inventoried, notices preserved, branding intact where a license requires it. We are your support-of-record and manage the upstream relationship.
Partner-supplied A commercial vendor's application, integrated by us. The vendor stands behind the code from day one; governance, deployment, and support run through us.
Customer-funded An app a customer funded that entered the shared catalog. Built once, maintained for everyone. Funding brings the app into existence, and the shared catalog keeps it maintained.

The catalog grows with you. Need something we don't stock? We build applications that belong in the catalog for everyone, and customer-funded builds are one of the ways it grows. Every app we add is one we'll maintain for every customer after you, and that shared maintenance is what keeps yours current too.
