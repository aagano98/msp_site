Solutions

One product, three reasons companies arrive.

Some buyers come for the team they've been missing, some for the bill, some because a regulator left them no choice. Underneath, all three want the same thing: AI they control, running without infrastructure they have to operate.

01 · No ML team required

Built for the IT team you already have.

You want AI under your own control, on your own terms, but there's no data-science or platform-engineering staff to build or run it, and no realistic path to hiring one. Today your options are a frontier seat with whatever terms come attached, or an integrator project nobody can operate after the integrator leaves.

Freehold is the third option: the assembled, governed, operated stack, run by the IT team you already have. If someone on your staff runs Google Workspace, Okta, or the M365 admin center, you have the person the console was designed for.

02 · The bill

For buyers who've seen the per-seat invoice.

The class of open-weight model that handles the bulk of everyday seat work sells an order of magnitude or more below frontier list prices. The governed-API posture puts those models behind your own gateway, under your own keys, with nothing to stand up.

Capture most of the price gap immediately, keep frontier models available through the same gateway for the work that genuinely needs them, and let your own metered usage decide if and when hardware ever makes sense.

The economics →
03 · The mandate

For organizations that were told "no shared cloud."

Data residency, single-tenancy mandates, client confidentiality, examiner findings: some organizations are contractually or regulatorily barred from sending their data to shared AI services, whatever it costs.

Freehold was designed from the start for you: the control plane inside your perimeter, sovereignty enforced by architecture and scored per app, a content-free audit built to hand to an examiner, and an isolation ladder that runs to a full air gap.

Worth noting

The reasons compound

Most regulated organizations of this size are also the ones with a five-person IT department keeping core systems running and no AI team at all. The mandate gets the project approved; the missing team is why it has to arrive as a finished product.

Where the fit is sharpest.

Freehold is industry-agnostic by construction, and vertical depth arrives as more apps in the catalog. These are the environments the product's constraints were designed against:

Financial services

Regional banks & credit unions

Examiner scrutiny, vendor-management requirements, and an IT department fully consumed keeping core systems running. A governed deployment with a provable audit trail fits the exam file as well as the roadmap.

Healthcare

Hospitals & health systems

PHI can't wander, and most systems operate with no dedicated data-science team. In-perimeter retrieval over policies and protocols, with content-free auditing, is the shape compliance can approve.

Legal

Law firms

Privilege makes shared-cloud AI a hard conversation. A firm-controlled deployment keeps that conversation inside the firm: retrieval over your own work product, matter-scoped access, and an audit the managing partner can read.

Public sector

State & local government

Residency requirements, procurement rules, and isolation postures up to a full air gap, on hardware the agency owns, with one accountable vendor.

The list above is a starting point. The product is industry-agnostic, and the catalog grows toward wherever our customers are. If your constraint is "our data can't leave" or "we have no one to run it," the fit conversation is worth having.
