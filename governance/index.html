Governance & Console

Built for the person who runs everything else.

The Freehold console is designed for the IT administrator who runs Google Workspace, Okta, or the M365 admin center. Not a dashboard for ML engineers you don't employ: an admin surface a generalist operates with confidence, because every screen answers a question their manager actually asks.

Request a walkthrough The enforcement underneath

Five questions. Five surfaces.

The console isn't organized around infrastructure concepts. It's organized around the five questions leadership asks the person running it:

Overview

"Is everything OK?"

One honest page: system health, what's reconciled, what changed recently, and anything that needs a decision. Green means measured-green, not hoped-green.

People & access

"Who can use what?"

Members, groups, and roles federated from your identity provider. Per-person budgets. Offboard someone and prove it happened, in one place.

Applications

"What are we offering?"

The deployed catalog and the shelf: every app under its real name, its assigned model, its sovereignty score, and its price. Nothing teased, nothing hidden.

Capacity & usage

"Are we sized right?"

What's hot, what's queued, who's using what, and where the load is trending, measured from the same metering that enforces budgets.

Safety & evidence

"Are we safe, and what changed?"

Egress posture, data-boundary labels per app, and the complete audit trail. Built to hand to an auditor, not just to reassure a manager.

Design principle

Measured against real jobs

The console is designed and tested against the concrete jobs a generalist must complete unaided: onboard a team, handle "the AI is slow," offboard a person and prove it, pass an audit, absorb new hardware.

Config-only control

Every action is a configuration change. Never a console command.

The admin console only ever writes configuration. It never reaches into the live system, and neither do you:

  • Propose, review, apply. Every change shows exactly what it will do before it applies. No surprise blast radius.
  • Versioned and signed. The configuration history is the audit trail: who changed what, when, and what the system looked like before.
  • Reconciled continuously. A proven GitOps engine holds the running system to its declared state. Anything tampered with is automatically restored.
  • Structurally enforced. "Admins can only write config" is proven by automated tests at the web layer, not asserted in a slide.

Budgets that enforce, not just report.

Cost control that arrives after the invoice isn't control. The Freehold gateway sits in front of every model call, in every posture, and enforces before serving.

Per-person budgets

Enforced at the gateway

Every request is checked against the member's budget before a model sees it. Usage totals accumulate from the same audit stream your reports read: one number, one source of truth.

Frontier allowances

Spend caps with graceful fallback

Give a group frontier access under your own key, capped by a per-person allowance. When it's spent, the app falls back automatically to a local model. Nobody's work stops; nobody's spend surprises you.

Metering

The numbers that decide posture

The same metering that enforces budgets measures your real demand shape, which is exactly what the on-prem-versus-API decision needs. Your posture recommendations come from your own numbers.

The audit trail

Content-free by construction.

Every model call writes an audit line: identity, application, model, decision, timestamp, token counts. Message content is never recorded, not as a policy that could change, but as a property of how the audit layer is built.

That's the version of observability a compliance officer can sign off on: complete enough to answer "who used what, when, and how much," and structurally incapable of becoming a surveillance log of what your employees wrote.

Identity

Your identity provider is the source of truth.

Members and groups federate from your IdP over OIDC. Access follows your existing joiner-mover-leaver process, and offboarding is provable: remove a person once, and the console shows you every app and model that access no longer reaches.

Roles in the console are scoped for real organizations: the person who sets budgets isn't necessarily the person who approves egress changes.

Honesty is a feature of the interface.

Admin consoles routinely invent certainty. Ours is built against stated principles that treat you as the adult in the room:

  • Every number carries its provenance. Measured, derived, or declared, labelled on screen. An estimate says it's an estimate.
  • A quantity we can't measure renders as a labelled absence. Never an invented figure, never a spinner pretending to be a number.
  • True names are mandatory. Models and applications appear under their real names, never marketing aliases. You're trusted to know what a model is.
  • The catalog is visibly a catalog. Transparent per-app pricing on the shelf, and nothing in the deployed experience locked, teased, or interrupted.
  • Sovereignty is scored, not asserted. Each application's data boundary is shown as configured: an app routed to an external model visibly reads lower than a pure in-perimeter one.
